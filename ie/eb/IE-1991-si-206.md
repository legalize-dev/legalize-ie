Days not to be treated as days of unemployment

15. (1) Article 7 of the Social Welfare (Disability, Unemployment and Marriage Benefit) Regulations, 1953 ( [S.I. No. 7 of 1953](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/1953/en/si/0007.html) ) is hereby amended by the substitution for paragraph (*b*) (inserted by article 3 of the Social Welfare (Disability, Unemployment and Marriage Benefit) (Amendment) (No. 2) Regulations, 1965 ( [S.I. No. 52 of 1965](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/1965/en/si/0052.html) ) and amendment by article 3 of the Social Welfare (Miscellaneous Provisions) (No. 2) Regulations, 1986 ( [S.I. No. 237 of 1986](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/1986/en/si/0237.html) )) of the following paragraphs—

- "(*b*) a day shall not be treated as a day of unemployment if an insured person—

    - (i) fails to prove to the satisfaction of the Minister in respect of that day that he is unemployed, capable of work and available for employment, or

    - (ii) is on that day following any occupation from which he derives any remuneration or profit, unless such occupation could ordinarily have been followed by him in addition to his usual employment and outside the ordinary working hours of that employment and either—

        - (I) The remuneration or profit therefrom for such day does not exceed £10, or, where the remuneration or profit is in respect of a period longer than a day, such remuneration or profit does not on the daily average exceed that amount, or

        - (II) not less than one hundred and seventeen employment contributions have been paid in respect of him in respect of either the period of three years immediately preceding that day or in respect of the last three complete contribution years immediately preceding that day;

( *c* ) where the provisions at clause (II) of sub-paragraph (ii) of paragraph (*b*) are satisfied as respects any day they shall continue to be regarded as satisfied in respect of any subsequent day which is in the same period of interruption of employment.".

- (2) ( *a* ) Subject to paragraph (*b*) of this sub-article, this article shall come into operation on the 25th day of July, 1991.

( *b* ) This article shall not apply in the case of any day of unemployment which forms part of a period of interruption of employment where the first day of unemployment commenced before the 25th day of July, 1991.

**EXPLANATORY NOTE.**

The [Social Welfare Act, 1991](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/1991/en/act/pub/0007/index.html) provided for increases in the maximum rates of social insurance payments arising from the 1991 Budget, with effect from the end of July, 1991. These Regulations provide for increases in the reduced rates of various social insurance payments from the end of July, 1991 and for an increase from £4 to £10 in the daily amount of earnings which a person may earn from a subsidiary occupation without affecting entitlement to unemployment benefit.

## Part II

Part II of the Regulations provides for increases in the rates of benefit payable under the occupational injuries benefits scheme—

— in respect of disablement gratuities appropriate to degrees of disablement assessed at 19 per cent or under,

— in respect of disablement pension payable in lieu of such gratuities,

— in respect of injury benefit payable to persons under the age of sixteen, and

— in respect of certain constant attendance allowances.

The new rates are payable from 29th July, 1991.

## Part III

Part III of the Regulations provides for increases in the existing scales of reduced rates of old age contributory pensions, widows' contributory pensions, retirement pensions and deserted wife's benefit where the contribution conditions for receipt of the maximum rates are not satisfied. These increases take effect from 26th July, 1991 in the case of old age contributory and widows' contributory pensions and from 25th July, 1991 in the case of retirement pensions and deserted wife's benefit.

## Part IV

Part IV of the Regulations provides for an increase from 26th July, 1991 in the reduced rates of old age (contributory) pension payable to certain persons who came back into insurance on the abolition of the earnings limit for liability for social insurance contributions in April, 1974, but who did not have a sufficient record of contributions to entitle them to a pension. Increases are also payable from 26th July, 1991 to widows who qualify for widow's (contributory) pension on this basis and similarly to deserted wives from 25th July, 1991.

## Part V

Part V of the Regulations provides for an increase from £4 to £10 in the daily amount of earnings which a person may earn from a subsidiary occupation without affecting entitlement to unemployment benefit. This daily earnings limit does not apply to persons who have a certain standard of PRSI contributions in a recent three-year period. These Regulations increase from 26 to 39 the yearly average number contributions required under this condition.

## PART VI

[Section 17](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/1991/en/act/pub/0007/sec0017.html#sec17) of the [Social Welfare Act, 1991](https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/1991/en/act/pub/0007/index.html) provides for a reduction from 48 to 39 in the number of paid or credited contributions required in the governing contribution year in order to qualify for the maximum rate of disability or unemployment benefit. Part VI of the Regulations provides for the revocation of obsolete regulatory provisions as a consequence of this measure.
